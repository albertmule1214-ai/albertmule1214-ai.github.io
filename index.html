Biography

Hi! I am Tingyu Zhu (Chinese name: 朱庭玉), a third-year undergraduate student at the School of Microelectronics, University of Science and Technology of China (USTC).

Under the guidance of Dr. Liheng Lou , I am studying wireline integrated circuits and high-speed interconnect systems, with interests in electrical links such as SerDes/CDR, optical communication circuits, TIAs and receiver front-ends, as well as silicon-photonic interconnects. My current research is focused on TIA circuit design for PCIe 7.0-related high-speed links.

I have received the Second Prize in RoboGame 2024 and the Second Prize in the Anhui Provincial Contest of the National Undergraduate Electronic Design Contest (NUEDC) 2025. Over the past month, my work has become more focused on receiver circuits for next-generation high-speed links. I am currently participating in a project of the SUGAR Network (Stanford Global Alliance for Design) , an international project-based learning program jointly conducted by USTC and HPI, in collaboration with Takeda.

🏆 Award: Anhui Provincial Second Prize
Team: Tingyu Zhu, Chengdong Xie, Muyang Zheng (3-person team)
Problem: Design an active RC filter with a target transfer function and build an exploration device capable of automatically identifying and reproducing the behavior of an unknown RLC circuit within limited time.
Technical Highlights:
• Analog front-end based on 2nd-order Sallen–Key low-pass topology with decoupled gain stage
• FPGA + MCU (STM32) co-design for programmable excitation, signal acquisition, and modeling
• Frequency-sweep–based system identification to characterize and reproduce unknown RLC responses
My Contributions:
• Independently designed, soldered, and debugged key analog modules (filter, attenuator, buffer)
• Proposed the overall solution using FPGA-based frequency sweeping for circuit modeling
• Authored the complete 8-page technical report covering design, implementation, and measurements
Results: Accurate magnitude response reproduction over the target band with ≤10% error; unknown RLC circuit successfully identified and modeled within 2 minutes.
